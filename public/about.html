How it works

Set it up once. Check it every morning.

No need to look up 5-digit stop codes. Just say how you go, like you'd tell a kaki.

  1. 1

    Describe your route

    I take 961 from the stop outside Blk 123, get off at Bt Panjang Int, walk across the road and take 975 to work.

  2. 2

    Check what it found

    kakiRoute finds the stop codes, works out which side of the road each bus leaves from, and estimates the walk and ride times. If two stops share a name, it asks you one short question. You check the form and save.

  3. 3

    Open it in the lift

    Your routes show live arrivals, in the order you saved them. By the time the doors open, you know which stop to walk to.

What you see

The maths you'd do in your head, done for you.

Amber when it's tight

Each transfer shows how many minutes you'd have spare. Under 2 minutes turns amber, so you know to walk faster or wait for the next one.

Walking transfers included

Get off, cross the road, catch the next bus. Each walk is part of the route, so the timing for the second bus is the one you can actually make.

Help me decide, when you want it

Your routes stay in the order you saved them. Can't choose? Tap Help me decide and it picks one of your own routes, weighing arrival time, tight transfers and rain where you'd walk.

Live data, plain arithmetic

Timings come from LTA's live bus arrivals (with how full each bus is) and LTA's train timetable (with how crowded the station is), refreshed every 15 seconds. You're warned when LTA reports a disruption or delay on your route. The timings themselves are simple addition. AI only steps in when you set up a route or tap Help me decide.

The mark

A stop, a short walk, and where you're going.

In Singlish a kaki is your buddy. In Malay it also means foot. The logo is a tiny route map of the trip the app helps with.

  • Purple stopWhere you start: the stop near your block.

  • Yellow footstepsThe walk. Kaki, the foot.

  • Red pinWhere you're going.
